World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026. US-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi settled a Russia-Ukraine prisoner swap of 1,000 to 1,500 a side, Kushner said, but no ceasefire or land deal (Reuters). Kushner and Witkoff head for Moscow. Russia hit six substations; three died in an Odesa market strike.

At COP31, UN chief Guterres urged windfall-profit taxes: the nine biggest firms earned $95 billion in a quarter, the Fund $600 million (UN News). He proposed a "climate justice compact" and warned warming could pass 2.5 degrees even if plans hold.

US prosecutors charged three men over Iran-backed plots, one aimed at RAF Fairford; Treasury chief Bessent was moved to a base after threats (New York Times). Iran's negotiator said Hormuz, shut 250 days, "will never return" to its pre-war state (Al Jazeera).

Four million Spanish workers struck over housing for the first time in 14 years, ahead of a 29 November election (Reuters). Rent averages 1,176 euros a month — 98.7 percent of a young worker's wage (Youth Council).

British police detained a US Army officer, 41, at a NATO corps near Gloucester, suspected of spying for Russia; Moscow denied any link (Reuters).

Alexander Darchiev, Moscow's envoy in Washington, died suddenly aged 66 (Reuters). A spokeswoman blamed a chronic illness; envoy since March 2025.

Kenya released its final Ebola patient, beginning a 42-day countdown to an Ebola-free declaration on 23 December (Reuters). The outbreak caused nine cases, seven recoveries, two deaths.

A new Israeli parliament took office with no government formed, Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc holding 53 of 120 seats (AP). Israeli strikes killed at least eight in Gaza; floods swamped 70,000 tents (Al Jazeera/CARE).

In Sudan's Blue Nile state, RSF drones killed nine displaced civilians travelling toward shelters in Damazin (Xinhua).

Nvidia directors met late Wednesday to discuss ousting chief executive Jensen Huang over a China spying scandal; shares dropped over 8% after hours (Reuters).
